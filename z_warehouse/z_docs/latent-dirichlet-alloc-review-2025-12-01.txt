## Chapter 10: The Dirichlet Distribution — Review

**File**: dirichlet-dstn.qmd | **Lines**: 308 | **Sections**: 6

---

### Core Concept

Mathematical foundation for LDA: the Dirichlet distribution as the conjugate prior to the multinomial, enabling efficient Bayesian inference over probability vectors. Builds from binomial/beta to multinomial/Dirichlet.

---

### Key Skills

- Understand categorical and multinomial distributions
- Recognize indicator vector (one-hot) encoding
- Apply Bayesian inference with conjugate priors
- Work with beta and Dirichlet distributions
- Interpret concentration parameters
- Derive posterior distributions algebraically

---

### Mathematical Prerequisites

**Substantial.** Requires: probability density functions, gamma function, Bayes' theorem, integration, simplex geometry. This is the most mathematically demanding chapter in the text section.

---

### Structure Assessment

**Logically organized mathematical exposition:**

|Section|Lines|Content|

The progression from K=2 (binomial/beta) to general K (multinomial/Dirichlet) is pedagogically sound.

### Strengths

1. **Clear motivation** (lines 51-57): Explicitly states this chapter prepares for understanding LDA

4. **Good visualization** (lines 150-189): Beta density plots for different shape parameters make the concentration concept tangible
5. **Concentration parameter intuition** (lines 281-291): Clear explanation that α₊ > K concentrates toward centroid, α₊ < K concentrates toward corners

### Knox Handoff (ML Preparation)

**Foundation for probabilistic ML.** The Dirichlet distribution appears in:

- Topic models (LDA)
- Bayesian naive Bayes
- Hierarchical Bayesian models
- Dirichlet process mixtures

The conjugacy concept is fundamental to Bayesian ML.
