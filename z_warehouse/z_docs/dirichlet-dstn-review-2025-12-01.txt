## Chapter 9: Latent Dirichlet Allocation — Review

**File**: latent-dirichlet-alloc.qmd | **Lines**: 882 | **Sections**: 8

---

### Core Concept

Deep dive into LDA as a probabilistic generative model for topic modeling. Expands on Chapter 8's introduction by presenting the full mathematical framework: documents as mixtures of topics, topics as distributions over vocabulary, and the Gibbs sampling algorithm for inference.

---

### Key Skills

- Understand topic models conceptually (documents as topic mixtures)
- Implement LDA via `lda::lda.collapsed.gibbs.sampler()`
- Interpret topic-term probability matrices (β)
- Evaluate topic models by comparing to known document sources
- Understand the generative model behind LDA
- Recognize the role of Dirichlet priors and concentration parameters

---

### Mathematical Prerequisites

**Moderate to substantial.** Requires: probability distributions, conditional probability, vectors over simplices, basic understanding of MCMC/Gibbs sampling. The notation is careful but dense.

---

### Structure Assessment

**Well-organized with clear progression:**

|Section|Lines|Content|
|---|---|---|
|Introduction|49-70|Topic models overview|
|Book Review Example|72-766|Animal Farm vs. Butter Battle Book|
|Mathematical Framework|768-875|Notation, generative model, Gibbs algorithm|
|Concluding Remarks|877-880|Summary|

The extended example (~700 lines) dominates the chapter, which is pedagogically appropriate—students see LDA in action before the mathematics.

---

### Strengths

1. **Clever toy example** (lines 72-84): Using Wikipedia plot summaries of "Animal Farm" and "The Butter Battle Book"—both Cold War allegories—creates a corpus where ground truth is known. Students can evaluate whether LDA recovers the true document sources.
2. **Code visibility** (lines 708, 753): Key visualization code is shown with `echo: true`
3. **Topic-term visualizations** (lines 706-723, 750-764): Bar charts of top terms per topic and log-ratio comparisons make abstract probability distributions concrete
4. **Clear notation section** (lines 770-813): Careful distinction between terms (vocabulary elements) and words (occurrences), corpus vs. document, topic as probability vector
5. **Generative model clearly stated** (lines 815-856): The three-step data generation process is explicit and interpretable
6. **Concentration parameter explanation** (lines 849-854): Good intuition for what α < 1 vs. α > 1 means for topic mixtures
7. **References to literature** (lines 855, 875): Cites @Antoniak_2023 for practical guidance, @Steyvers_Griffiths_2007 for algorithm details

---

### Weaknesses

1. **Code mostly hidden** (line 8: `echo = FALSE`): Despite a few visible blocks, the actual LDA implementation—the call to `lda.collapsed.gibbs.sampler()`—is not shown
2. **Data sourcing unclear**: The Wikipedia plot summaries are stored as local text files. Students can't easily replicate without those files.
3. **No hyperparameter tuning**: The chapter sets K=2 (known to be correct) but doesn't discuss how to choose K in practice
4. **Gibbs algorithm underexplained** (lines 857-875): The description is accurate but brief. Students may not understand _why_ Gibbs sampling works or what "collapsed" means.
5. **No model evaluation metrics**: Beyond visual inspection, how do we know the topics are good? Perplexity, coherence scores not mentioned.
6. **Missing connection to Chapter 4**: The Gibbs sampler is MCMC, which was introduced in Chapter 4. A cross-reference would strengthen coherence.
7. **Typo** (line 869): Equation label `{#eq-log-ratio-topic-term}` is reused from an earlier equation—should be unique

---

### EDA Connection

**Implicit.** The topic-term visualizations are exploratory, but the chapter doesn't frame LDA as an EDA tool for discovering thematic structure in text corpora.

### Overall Assessment

**This is a solid deep-dive chapter.** The Animal Farm / Butter Battle Book example is creative and effective—students see LDA applied to a problem where they can verify the results. The mathematical framework is careful and complete.
