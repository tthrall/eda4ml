## Chapter 6: Linear Regression — Review

**File**: la-intro.qmd | **Lines**: 2,018 | **Sections**: ~15

---

### Core Concept

Linear regression as orthogonal projection. This chapter presents the geometry of least squares regression, showing that the fitted values y^\hat{y} y^​ are the orthogonal projection of yy y onto the column space of the feature matrix XX X. This is the conceptual bridge between statistical regression and linear algebra.

---

### Key Skills

- Formulate linear regression in matrix notation
- Distinguish row perspective (observations) from column perspective (features)
- Center data to eliminate intercept terms
- Derive the normal equations
- Understand least squares as orthogonal projection
- Interpret the projection matrix P=X(X⊤X)−1X⊤P = X(X^\top X)^{-1}X^\top P=X(X⊤X)−1X⊤
- Connect covariance to inner products

---

### Mathematical Prerequisites

**Moderate to substantial.** Requires: matrix multiplication, transpose, inverse, inner products, norms, vector spaces, subspaces, span. Students need comfort with linear algebra notation. The chapter introduces these concepts but assumes some prior exposure.

### Strengths

1. **Row vs. column perspective** (lines 1838-1843): Explicit articulation of this duality is excellent. Many students never learn this distinction.
2. **Galton 3D visualization** (lines 98-179): Effective use of `scatterplot3d` to show regression plane with residual line segments
3. **Centering treatment** (lines 783-1018): Thorough explanation of why centering eliminates the intercept and produces a proper subspace
4. **Projection matrix properties** (lines 1078-1098): Clear proof that P is idempotent and symmetric
5. **Comprehensive exercises** (lines 1846-2018): Outstanding exercise set covering concepts, calculations, programming, and advanced theory. The progression from hand calculations (1D projection) to geometric insight (correlation as cosine) is well-designed.
6. **MNIST and BLI examples**: Shows that linear algebra applies beyond simple regression—connects to classification and high-dimensional data

---

### Weaknesses

1. **Code hidden** (line 8): Students don't see `lm()`, `scatterplot3d()`, or matrix operations in R
2. **Multinomial logistic section** (lines 373-484): This is a digression. The detailed derivation of softmax probabilities is valuable but interrupts the linear algebra narrative. Could be moved to Chapter 7 (classification) or an appendix.
3. **No class exercises**: Unlike earlier chapters, there are no in-class team exercises—only end-of-chapter exercises
4. **Missing "why this matters for ML"**: The chapter never explicitly states that linear regression is the foundation for many ML methods (ridge, lasso, neural networks as generalized linear models)
5. **BLI data sourcing**: The OECD API issue from your package development session may affect reproducibility

---

### EDA Connection

**Implicit.** The Galton data visualization and 3D scatter plots are EDA, but the chapter doesn't frame them that way. A sentence connecting regression diagnostics (residual plots, leverage) to EDA would strengthen the link.

---

### Knox Handoff (ML Preparation)

**Strong foundation.** The projection perspective is essential for understanding:

- Ridge/lasso regression (modified projections)
- Kernel methods (implicit feature spaces)
- Neural network layers (sequential linear transformations)
- Gradient descent (iterative approximation of projection)

A brief "Looking Ahead" paragraph noting these connections would be valuable.
