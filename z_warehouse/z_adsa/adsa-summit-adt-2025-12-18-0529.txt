Dear Micaela,

I hope this finds you well. You may remember me from the ADSA AI Working Group in 2024, where I participated in discussions on mapping work roles to the data lifecycle alongside Victor Piotrowski, Barney Maccabe, and others. I'm writing to request an invitation to the 2026 Leadership Summit in Tucson.

I recently retired from NSA after ten years, but I remain actively engaged in data science education and workforce development. I believe I could contribute a perspective that may be underrepresented at the Summit: that of someone who has hired and developed data scientists in both the public and private sectors, and who has worked on both sides of the skills-marketplace problem—defining what employers need and assessing what candidates bring.

**What I would bring to the Summit:**

*Workforce standards development.* At NSA, I led the effort to update the Intelligence Community's Data Science Competency Resource Guide, released by ODNI in 2023. I subsequently led development of behaviorally-anchored assessments aligned with those standards—work that required translating competency frameworks into measurable outcomes. This experience speaks directly to the "Student Readiness and Workforce Development" session theme.

*Cross-sector hiring perspective.* Before NSA, I hired data scientists in Silicon Valley tech firms and in insurance/financial services in New York. The challenge of matching employer needs to applicant qualifications looks different in each sector, and different again in government. I've seen what works and what doesn't when competency frameworks meet actual hiring decisions.

*Curriculum-to-workforce alignment.* I've recently completed an online textbook, "Exploratory Data Analysis for Machine Learning," based on internal training I developed at NSA. The book advocates for hybrid workflows that integrate classical statistical methods with AI-based tools—a pedagogical stance that seems relevant to the "Data Science and AI Integration Across the Curriculum" session. I would be glad to share the book with Summit participants if useful.

*Skills marketplace research.* Through collaboration with Workera.ai on skills intelligence platforms, I've explored how competency ontologies can make the labor market more efficient for both employers and job-seekers. This connects to ADSA's broader mission of providing transparency for students and employers navigating the landscape of data science credentials.

I recognize that the Summit is by invitation only and that space is limited. If there's a way I can contribute—whether through a session, an unconference talk, or simply informed participation—I would welcome the opportunity.

Thank you for considering my request. I'm happy to provide any additional information that would be helpful.

All the best,
Tony
