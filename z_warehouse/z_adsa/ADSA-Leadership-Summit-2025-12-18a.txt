The 2026 Leadership Summit will be hosted by the University of Arizona on March 16 - 18, 2026 in Tucson, AZ.

About the Summit
The Data Science and AI Leadership Summit convenes around 100 distinguished thought leaders, educators, and innovators to discuss the implementation and management of AI and Data Science in education and research. The goals of the Summit are: to form an academic community for data science and AI; to share best practices where they face similar challenges and opportunities; and to take collective responsibility in preparing next-generation data and AI professionals to contribute in the best interests of society. Attendance at the Leadership Summit is by invitation only; email micaela@alliance4datascience.ai if you're interested.

Program will include:

  - Session Deep Dives (see session descriptions below)
  - New Member Welcome talks
  - Unconference talks
  - FREE optional participation in University of Arizona's "Universities As AI Stewards For Arizona And Beyond" Summit on Day 3
  - Special interest meetings and focused discussions
  - Networking, and more!

Session:
title: 
  - Data Science and AI Integration Across the Curriculum
organizers: 
  - Mario Banuelos (CSU Fresno), Rachel Saidi (Montgomery College)
abstract:
  - This session will focus on the process of designing coherent, forward-looking curricula that leverage data science as a foundation of AI. The discussion will include strategies for aligning core student learning outcomes that can translate and articulate across the landscape of academic institutions in the face of rapidly evolving technologies. As data science is central to AI education, panelists and participants will explore how AI concepts and tools can be integrated into data science coursework to be both specific enough to provide workforce-aligned skills and knowledge and also open-ended enough to adapt to the future evolution of computational technology (algorithm design, data lifecycle principles, and ethics). This interactive session will also include discussion of the systemic need for professional development to prepare faculty to teach AI-infused content responsibly and effectively.

Session:
title: 
  - Who Leads AI Strategy in Higher Ed: Roles, Responsibilities, and Navigating Challenges
organizers: 
  - Noah Smith (University of Washington)
abstract:
  - As universities move beyond research-focused AI activity, new leadership positions are emerging to guide strategic integration across academic programs, administration, operations, and external partnerships. This session will examine how these roles influence university-wide planning, support curriculum and policy development, and promote responsible innovation. Participants will discuss how leadership structures can enable cross-department collaboration while responding to external pressures from industry, government, and philanthropy.  The session will consider how institutions are determining where AI leadership should reside, whether within existing structures such as data science institutes or technology offices, or through the creation of new entities, and what factors inform these decisions. It will also address resource planning, infrastructure needs, and governance models related to AI.  Finally, the discussion will reflect on the distinctive role universities can play as AI’s influence expands across sectors, including their responsibilities related to knowledge development, policy, and public understanding.


Session:
title: 
  - Student Readiness and Workforce Development in this Era of AI
organizers: 
  - Mahesh Chaudhari (University of San Francisco)
abstract:
  - The rise of AI is transforming career pathways and redefining the very nature of the workforce. As AI reshapes the job market and entry-level roles, academic programs face the challenge of preparing students for evolving workforce realities. Generative AI is now widely adopted as part of higher education, with students increasingly relying on these tools to accelerate learning and productivity. This panel will address how academic programs can equip students for employability in an AI-driven world and navigate future disruptions in early career roles, career ladders, and workplace expectations. The panel will also address how academic programs can meaningfully connect student experience to workforce needs while maintaining intellectual integrity and pedagogical best practices. Panelists will share approaches for developing applied learning experiences, showcasing student capabilities to employers, and fostering partnerships that connect educational programs to the realities of an AI-transformed workplace.

Session:
title: 
  - Engaging with the Broader Campus Community and Public Around Responsible AI
organizers: 
  - Penny Atkins (Utah), Meredith Broussard (NYU)
abstract:
  - AI has generated excitement as well as doom and gloom. How can we communicate better with the public about AI realities? This session addresses the need for data science and AI institutes, departments, centers, and colleges to engage in campus and community-wide dialog, education, and outreach about the responsible use of AI. During this panel, we will discuss the role that data science and AI units on campus should and do have in providing guidance and best practices to our colleagues and broader community. Given the traditional role of universities as educators for both students and the community and the unique opportunities presented with AI, we will evaluate the role data science and AI units at our universities play in shaping the broader conversation about AI in society. We will then discuss effective strategies on how academics can meaningfully engage with our communities and reflect on the unique responsibilities that academics hold in shaping responsible use of AI and public discourse around AI.
